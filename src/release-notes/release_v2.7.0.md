## A swatch instead of two words

Until now the colour and the finish of a material were text, and only text.
"Black", "silk" — you had to read them, one row at a time, to find the spool you
meant.

The overview now has a **Look** column showing both at once: the colour as a
small square, the finish drawn on top of it. Matte is a fine grain, glossy has
two highlight streaks, carbon a woven pattern, silk a soft sheen across the
face, and glow a halo. The swatch also appears on the material page, on the
cards you see on a phone, and in a friend's inventory.

The words stay where they were. The swatch sits next to them, it does not
replace them.

**The pattern is never invisible.** Its colour is worked out from the colour
underneath: white filament gets dark highlight streaks, black filament light
ones. There is no combination where the finish disappears into its own colour.

## Colours filahub already knows — and yours

Common colours are recognised out of the box, in German and in English, however
you capitalise them: black, white, natural, grey, anthracite, silver, gold,
copper, red, orange, yellow, green, turquoise, blue, navy, purple, magenta,
pink, brown, beige and a few more. The same goes for the finishes already
suggested in the material form.

For everything else there is a new page, **Colours & finishes**:

- **Your own colours.** Give the name as it appears on your material and pick
  the colour code — from the colour picker, or typed in if the manufacturer
  gives you one. A colour of your own beats the built-in one, so you can also
  correct a "red" that is not your red.
- **Your own finishes.** Your name, one of the built-in patterns. Anything a
  manufacturer invents — "Sparkle", "Seidenmatt" — can point at the pattern that
  comes closest.

When a colour has no code, the swatch shows a neutral hatched square rather than
guessing. Nothing is invented from a name: "fire red" is not going to come out
green. In the material form a colour that is not yet known offers to be added
right there, without losing what you have typed so far.

Deleting a colour never touches your materials. They keep the name; only the
swatch falls back to the hatched square.

In an organisation the colours belong to the organisation, so everyone sees the
same stock the same way.

## A form that stays in its columns

A long entry used to push the material form out of shape. The name of a
container, or the button offering to store a colour, reached into the field
beside it and covered its label; on a phone the whole form slid sideways and the
right-hand end of every field was out of reach. Fields now keep to their column
and shorten what does not fit into it.

The suggestion lists in the form — material type, manufacturer, colour, finish —
are no longer cut off at the lower edge of the form; they stay whole and move out
of the way when there is no room below the field. And every drop-down is now as
wide as the field it belongs to, here and on the other pages, so a form reads as
one column of equal fields again.
