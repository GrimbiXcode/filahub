title: One stock, several people
---

Until now filahub assumed one stock belongs to one person. That fits a workshop
at home and nothing else: a company, a university print hub or a makerspace has
one shelf of filament and a dozen people reaching into it.

Organizations are that shelf. Create one, give it stores, and everyone you let
in works on the same stock — not a copy, not a shared view, the stock itself.

## Switching between your own stock and a shared one

A new switcher sits at the top of the sidebar, above the store switcher. It has
"Personal" and one entry per organization you belong to, and it decides what
every page shows: your dashboard, your stores, your container types, your
dryboxes, your search.

Nothing mixes. Your personal stock stays exactly as private as it was, and
switching back shows it unchanged. Each side remembers its own selected store,
so you do not land in the wrong place after switching.

## Four levels, and what each may do

Not everyone in a workshop should be able to delete a store. So membership comes
at one of four levels, and each includes the ones below it:

| Level  | May                                                                                  |
| ------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| View   | see and search the stock, the stores, container types and dryboxes                   |
| Weigh  | also weigh — the everyday action of booking material out                             |
| Edit   | also add, change, delete and import material; maintain containers and dryboxes       |
| Manage | also create and delete stores, invite people, assign levels, rename the organization |

Buttons you are not allowed to use are not shown, rather than shown greyed out.
A view-level member sees the stock and no weighing button; raise them to weigh
and the button appears on their next visit.

Creating a store is a manage-level action even though adding material is not. A
store is structure — deleting one affects everyone.

Weighing has one wrinkle worth knowing. Misread the scale and you can delete the
weigh-in you just entered — that is the everyday correction, and needing a
colleague for it would make the weigh level useless. What you cannot do at that
level is reach back into the history: only the most recent entry counts, and
only for fifteen minutes. After that, removing a weigh-in takes the edit level.
A weighing history is the record of how the stock got to where it is, and it is
not something a slip of the hand should be able to erase.

## Two ways in

**A join code** (`ORG-…`) that anyone can enter to join straight away. You
decide which level it hands out, and it can never hand out "manage" — a code
gets passed around and pinned to a wall, and whoever found it should not end up
running the place. Switch it off or generate a fresh one at any time; the old
one stops working immediately.

**A personal invitation** by friend code or Telegram name, at whichever level
you pick. It takes effect only when the invited person accepts, so nobody ends
up in an organization without doing something themselves. Open invitations are
listed for administrators and can be withdrawn.

An invitation is only valid while the person who sent it still administers the
organization. If they are removed or stepped down in the meantime, the
invitation lapses — an administrator who has left should not still be able to
hand out access.

## What members see of each other

Everyone in an organization sees the other members' display name and Telegram
username, the same two fields as in the friends list. This goes further than
sharing with a friend, where a curated subset of each material is passed on:
here you are all looking at the same stock and, from the weigh level upwards,
changing it.

Two things worth knowing before you join one:

- **What you record in an organization's store stays with the organization** if
  you leave or delete your account. Those entries do not carry your name — we do
  not keep a record of who booked what — so there is nothing of yours in them.
- **An organization's stock is not part of your data export.** Your memberships
  and invitations are.

There is always at least one administrator. Stepping down or leaving is refused
while you are the last one — appoint someone else first. If you delete your
account outright, the longest-standing member takes over and is told about it;
if there is nobody left, the organization goes with you.

## What stays personal

Sharing with friends is unchanged, and a store belonging to an organization
cannot be shared with friends — one administrator should not be able to open the
whole group's stock to outsiders.

The container catalogue also stays yours: what you hide in it and what you
propose to it is your business, not the organization's. Copying a catalogue
entry does follow the switcher, so copying while working in an organization adds
the container type there, where you can see it.
